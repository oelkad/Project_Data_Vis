# Demo video script (≈3 min 45)

Record the browser at full width, sidebar open. Speak calmly; one idea per scene.
Timings are targets — stay between 2 and 4 minutes.

---

**0:00 – 0:25 · Hook (page 1 · Why it matters)**
*Show the title and the author card, then the "Off track for 2030" chart; hover the 2024 point.*
> "Hi, I'm Othmane El Kadiri, Data and AI at Efrei. In 2024, 3,193 people died on the roads of metropolitan France — nine a day. That is barely 1.6 % fewer than in 2019. Europe's goal is to halve road deaths by 2030; this dashed line is the path to get there. In 2024 we were almost 700 deaths above it. The question of this app is simple: where should the next effort go?"

**0:25 – 0:40 · Audience & data**
*Scroll to "Who this story is for" and the blue "How to read it" box.*
> "It is built for local road-safety decision-makers. The data is the national police file of injury accidents, from data.gouv.fr: five years, four tables joined together — 269 thousand accidents and 612 thousand people."

**0:40 – 1:05 · The big picture (page 2)**
*Point at the KPI row, then the monthly chart, then the urban/rural bars.*
> "These figures follow the sidebar filters. The monthly chart shows the lockdown dips in grey, then a plateau. And the first key insight: rural roads carry about a third of accidents but two thirds of deaths."
*Switch "Road environment" to **Rural** — the KPIs change — then back to **All**.*

**1:05 – 2:00 · Where (page 3, tab Where)**
*In the sidebar, set "Metric for the map and ranking" to **Lethality**. The map and the top 10 update.*
> "Now the map shows lethality: deaths per 100 accidents. The deadliest departments are rural — Jura, Haute-Saône, Creuse. This scatter makes it obvious: Paris has the most accidents but the lowest lethality."
*Hover Paris, then a rural bubble. Scroll to "Which roads?" and click **Speed limit**.*
> "80 km/h roads: 14 % of accidents, 36 % of deaths."
*Scroll to the correlation matrix and point at the first column.*
> "The correlation matrix compares the 96 departments: the deadliest ones have more crashes into trees and poles, more 80 km/h roads and more unlit roads — rural factors that all go together. It's a correlation, not a cause."
*Scroll back up, click a department on the map, then scroll down to the drill-down.*
> "Clicking a department opens it here: its key figures and every fatal accident on the map."

**2:00 – 2:30 · Who (tab Who)**
> "Car occupants are half of the dead. But once involved in an accident, pedestrians, motorcyclists and cyclists die about twice as often. Two age peaks: young adults, and the over-75s — a third of them pedestrians."
*Scroll to the small multiples, hover E-scooter.*
> "And a new risk: e-scooter deaths went from 9 to 47 in four years."

**2:30 – 2:50 · When (tab When)**
*Show the grid in "Killed per 100 accidents", then switch to "Injury accidents".*
> "Accidents peak at the evening rush hour, but the deadliest hours are at night, especially at weekends: 2.3 times more lethal."

**2:50 – 3:20 · What would it take? (page 4)**
*Click **Rural-roads plan**, then **Both plans**.*
> "Here is the what-if. Cutting rural car deaths alone is not enough. Only acting on rural roads and vulnerable users together gets close to the goal — and even this ambitious plan covers about 60 % of the way."

**3:20 – 3:45 · Takeaways & transparency (page 5, then Data quality)**
> "The takeaways turn this into actions: rural roads, the night, unprotected users. Finally, the data-quality page lists every cleaning step and check — our totals match the official ONISR figures exactly — and the limits: police data under-count light injuries and there is no traffic data, so these are associations, not causes. Thank you."
