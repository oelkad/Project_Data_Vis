# Storyboard — "Road deaths in France: the curve has stopped falling"

**Audience:** local road-safety decision-makers (préfecture coordinators, conseils départementaux); secondary: citizens/journalists.
**Goal of the reader:** know where to focus the next road-safety actions in their territory.
**Narrative arc:** Hook → Context → Insights (Where / Who / When) → What it would take → Implications → Transparency.

| # | Screen (page) | Question answered | Headline (one sentence) | Visuals & interactions | Why this chart |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Why it matters** (hook) | Is road safety still improving? | 3,193 deaths in 2024, only −1.6 % vs 2019: France is ~690 deaths above the path to the EU 2030 goal. | Author / course card; 3 KPI tiles; line chart *actual vs straight path to −50 %* with gap annotation and COVID band. | A reference line turns a flat curve into a gap: the reader *sees* "off track". |
| 1b | Who / questions / how to read | — | — | Text: audience, 3 questions, "How to read it" box (filters, definition of lethality, data source). | Sets expectations and vocabulary before the numbers. |
| 2 | **The big picture** (context) | How have accidents and deaths evolved? | Over 2020–2024, 15,112 deaths in 253,740 accidents; 67 % of deaths on rural roads. | **KPI header tied to the sidebar filters** (accidents, killed, hospitalised, deaths/100 acc., rural share; deltas vs previous year); 2-panel monthly line chart with lockdown bands; grouped bars urban vs rural per year. | Two aligned panels instead of a dual axis; bands explain the 2020 dips so they are not read as progress. |
| 3a | **Where** | Where are accidents the deadliest? | Outside towns, one accident in 9 kills; in towns one in 33. | Department choropleth (metric chosen in sidebar; click = drill-down); scatter accidents vs lethality (log x, bubble = deaths, colour = rural share, average line); top-10 ranking; *share of accidents vs share of deaths* by road type / speed limit / collision / intersection; **correlation matrix** of 11 department-level indicators (Pearson); drill-down KPIs + point map of fatal accidents. | The scatter shows the key non-obvious pattern: most accidents ≠ most deadly. Share-vs-share bars expose over-representation (80 km/h roads: 14 % → 36 %). The correlation matrix shows which factors move together with lethality — and with each other. |
| 3b | **Who** | Who dies, who is most exposed? | Car occupants are half of the dead, but pedestrians, motorcyclists and cyclists die ~2× as often once involved. | Two bar charts (count vs rate per 1,000 involved, highlight ≥1.5× car); age pyramid by sex; small multiples of deaths per year by road user. | Count vs rate side by side avoids the "cars are the problem" misreading; small multiples reveal the e-scooter rise without a cluttered legend. |
| 3c | **When** | When are accidents deadliest? | Weekend nights: 11.3 deaths per 100 accidents, 2.3× weekday daytime. | Weekday × hour heatmap (toggle accidents / killed / lethality); share bars by lighting; monthly small multiples for 4 road users. | Heatmap shows both rush-hour volume and night lethality with one toggle. |
| 4 | **What would it take?** (what-if) | Which efforts would reach the 2030 goal? | A 49 % cut is needed; no single group is big enough. | 6 sliders on non-overlapping groups, 3 preset plans + reset, waterfall from 2024 deaths to the scenario with the goal line, progress bar, assumptions listed below. | Turns insights into a decision tool; partitioned groups make reductions additive and honest. |
| 5 | **Takeaways** | So what? | Rural roads, the night and unprotected users concentrate the risk. | 7 numbered insights, implications table (finding → where to act → levers), limits and next steps. | Closes the arc with actions for the stated audience. |
| 6 | **Data quality & method** | Can I trust it? | Cleaned data reproduce the official death toll exactly. | Source, validation checks, cleaning log, limitations, privacy, reproducibility. | Transparency about methods and limits. |

**Global controls (sidebar):** years, territory, region, urban/rural, metric for the map & ranking.
**Colour logic:** blue = main series, orange = contrast/emphasis (rural, deaths, highlighted), grey = context; single-hue blue ramp for maps and heatmaps; blue/red for the correlation matrix.
